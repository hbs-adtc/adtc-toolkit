# ADTC Club Context

Use this to shape questions and copy. It is background, not a script.

## Mission
Make Harvard the epicenter of deep tech commercialization in Boston, and eventually the world. Boston is a deep tech center, and HBS sits next to MIT with deep university ties to the capital that hard tech needs.

## Pillars
Robotics, Future of Compute, Biotech, Climate Tech, Manufacturing, Materials Science. Each pillar has a head and an events lead who own its events.

## Event goals
- At least one flagship event per pillar per semester, owned by that pillar's head and events lead.
- Anchor formats: fireside chats, panels, workshops, office hours, small-group dinners, lab or office visits.
- More events are welcome, but flagships beat one-offs. RC year is busy, so each event should be worth the time.

## Audience
- Members are mostly engineers with experience at companies like Apple, Amazon, Tesla, SpaceX and Waymo.
- More than half want to found a deep tech company. Top interests: robotics, manufacturing and supply chain, energy and climate.
- Events are primarily for students, faculty and staff of Boston-area universities. When oversubscribed, prioritize entry for people with university IDs. Say so on the Luma for anything likely to fill up.

## What works
- Educational content with a concrete takeaway (for example, advice on scaling a robotics company) draws better than a pure speaker panel. Push every event toward something attendees can use.
- Big names draw registrations fast. A recent panel drew 400+ registrations, so plan for overflow and a no-show rate on big events.

## Visual standard
- Use the ADTC design system file for Luma headers, flyers and slides, not the look of the club website.
- Speaker photos on covers are background-removed, black and white cutouts placed directly on a white background.
- Use the actual ADTC logo, not the gear/robot-arm mark from the website.
