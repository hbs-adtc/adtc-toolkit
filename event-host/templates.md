# Templates

Starting points. Always rewrite with the real speaker, topic and goal. Keep the voice short, specific and plain. No em dashes.

## 1. Warm intro request (to Design Club or a connector)

> Hi [Name], I'm planning an ADTC [format] on [topic] for [month]. The goal is for students to leave with [takeaway]. [Speaker] would be a great fit because [one specific reason]. Do you know them well enough to make an intro? Happy to send a blurb you can forward.
>
> [First name]

## 2. LinkedIn cold note (under 300 characters)

> Hi [Name], I run [pillar] events for the Automation & Deep Tech Club at HBS. Your work on [specific thing] is exactly what our members (mostly engineers, many future founders) want to learn from. Open to a 45-min [format] on campus in [month]?

## 3. Speaker logistics email (after they say yes)

Subject: ADTC [format] on [date]: logistics

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks again for doing this. Here is what we have so far:
> - **Date and time:** [date], [start] to [end]
> - **Location:** [room], HBS campus. [Parking or arrival note]
> - **Format:** [format], about [N] minutes plus Q&A
> - **Audience:** ~[headcount] students, mostly engineers, many planning to start deep tech companies
> - **Goal:** attendees leave with [takeaway]
>
> Could we find 30 minutes in the next two weeks to shape the content? If you have slides or notes, send them over beforehand and I'll come with suggestions.
>
> [First name]

## 4. Speaker prep meeting agendas

**Meeting 1 (T-5 to T-4 weeks): shape**
- Confirm the goal and audience
- What is the one thing they want students to remember?
- Their story arc: 3 to 4 moments or lessons
- Ask for slides or notes before meeting 2
- Logistics: A/V needs, arrival time, photo okay, recording okay

**Meeting 2 (T-2 weeks): sharpen**
- Walk through their slides. Cut background, expand the parts with specific numbers, decisions and mistakes.
- Agree on the fireside questions or workshop run of show
- Confirm logistics and send the final Luma link

## 5. Luma copy

**Title:** [Speaker] on [specific topic], not a generic theme
**Hook (one line):** what attendees will be able to do or understand afterward
**Description:**
> [2 to 3 sentences on the speaker's most relevant experience, with one specific number or result.]
>
> In this [format], we'll cover:
> - [Takeaway 1]
> - [Takeaway 2]
> - [Takeaway 3]
>
> [Food note.] Open to students, faculty and staff of Boston-area universities. If we're oversubscribed, priority goes to attendees with a university ID.

## 9. Thank-you to the speaker (T+1 day)

> Hi [Name], thank you for yesterday. [One specific moment or line that landed with students.] [N] people came and the questions kept going after we wrapped. Here are a few photos. If you're ever open to a smaller dinner or office hours with members, we'd love to have you back.
>
> [First name]

## 10. Fireside question set structure

Write 10 to 12 questions in this arc. Use the speaker's real history; no question should work for any other speaker.
1. **Origin** (1 to 2): a specific decision point in their path
2. **The hard part** (3 to 4): the technical or commercial problem that almost broke them, with numbers
3. **Lessons for founders** (2 to 3): what they'd tell an engineer starting a company in this space today
4. **The view ahead** (1 to 2): where the field is going and where the openings are
5. **Lightning round** (optional, 3 quick ones)
Leave 15 to 20 minutes for audience Q&A.
